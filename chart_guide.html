Control Chart Selection Guide

Variable (measured) and attribute (counted) charts: which one to use, where, and practice data in Stat Studio.

Open Stat Studio

1. Two kinds of data

Variable data measured

A number on a continuous scale: length, weight, time, temperature, pressure, cost.

Gives more information per reading, so it needs smaller samples and detects shifts sooner.

Attribute data counted

A count of pass/fail items or of defects.

Defective = a whole item is bad (1 item, good or bad). Defect = one flaw; an item can have many.
Example: a shirt with 3 stains is 1 defective and has 3 defects.

2. Which chart? Follow the questions

  • Is the data measured or counted?
    • Measured →How many readings in each subgroup?
      • 1 (one reading at a time)I-MR
      • 2 to 8Xbar-R
      • 9 or moreXbar-S
    • Counted →Counting defective items, or defects?
      • Defective items →Is the sample size constant?
        • YesNP
        • NoP
      • Defects →Is the area / opportunity constant?
        • YesC
        • NoU

4. Variable charts and where to use them

I-MR n = 1

One reading per time period; the data are slow or costly to get.

  • Viscosity of each batch of paint
  • Monthly electricity consumption of a plant
  • Concentration in a process tank, tested once per shift
  • Time taken to process each loan application
  • Strength from a destructive test (1 piece per lot)
Practice: Diameter column (Stat › Control Charts › I-MR).

Xbar-R n = 2 to 8

Small rational subgroups taken together from the same stream.

  • Shaft diameter: 5 consecutive parts every hour
  • Fill weight of 4 bottles every 30 minutes
  • Lab turnaround time: 5 reports per shift
  • Paint film thickness on 4 panels per batch
  • Cycle time of 5 consecutive machine cycles each hour
Practice: Diameter column, subgroup size 5.

Xbar-S n ≥ 9

Large subgroups, where the range wastes information.

  • Tablet weight: 10 tablets from the press every 15 minutes
  • Bore diameter of the 10 cavities in a mould, each shot
  • Tensile strength of 12 coupons per spool lot
  • Fabric thickness at 10 points across a roll
  • Fill volume of a 12-head filler, measured together
Practice: Diameter column, subgroup size 10 (or 5).

5. Attribute charts and where to use them

P chart defective, n varies

Fraction of items that fail; the sample size changes.

  • Fraction of invoices with errors each day (volume varies)
  • Proportion of castings rejected per lot
  • Share of patients readmitted within 30 days, per month
  • Fraction of late deliveries per week
  • Proportion of orders returned by customers per day
Practice: Rejects with Lot_Size. Sample 18 is out of control.

NP chart defective, n constant

Number of defective items in a fixed-size sample.

  • Defective bulbs in a fixed sample of 100 per shift
  • Leaking bottles in 100 checked every hour
  • Incorrect entries in an audit of 50 forms
  • Failed units in a 30-piece pass/fail test per batch
  • Non-conforming parts in 200 inspected per lot
Practice: Faulty column, subgroup size 100. Sample 12 is out of control.

C chart defects, area constant

Number of defects on equal-sized units.

  • Scratches on each painted panel of the same size
  • Bubbles in each 1 m² glass sheet
  • Typing errors per fixed-length document
  • Soldering defects per circuit board of one type
  • Customer complaints per week, or near-misses per month
Practice: Flaws column. Sample 9 is out of control.

U chart defects, size varies

Defects per unit when the amount inspected changes.

  • Flaws per metre of cable, with varying lengths checked
  • Errors per report of different page counts
  • Bugs per 1000 lines in modules of different size
  • Falls per 1000 patient-days, with varying patient-days
  • Paint defects per car body in batches of different size
Practice: Errors with Pages. Sample 21 is out of control.

6. Test yourself: choose the chart

A. Weight of 5 pills taken from the press every hour

Xbar-R: measured, subgroup of 5.

B. Percentage of orders shipped late each day; daily orders vary

P chart: defective orders, changing sample size.

C. Bubbles on each 1 m² glass sheet

C chart: defects, constant area.

D. Furnace temperature logged once per hour

I-MR: one reading at a time.

E. Failed units among a fixed 50-piece sample from each lot

NP chart: defective items, constant sample size.

F. Errors per 1000 lines of code in modules of different size

U chart: defects, changing amount inspected.

G. Fill volume of all 12 filling heads, measured together every 15 minutes

Xbar-S: measured, subgroup of 12.

7. Good practice

Control limits are not specification limits. Limits come from the process data, specs come from the customer. Never draw one on the other.
  • Stable first, capable second. Check the control chart before quoting Cp or Cpk.
  • Rational subgroups. Take items produced close together, so variation inside a subgroup is common-cause only.
  • Use 20 to 25 subgroups to set trial limits, and reset them only after a verified process change.
  • Investigate special causes. A flagged point means find out what changed; adjusting for common-cause noise makes things worse.
  • Count correctly. Defectives go on P or NP, defects on C or U. A mix-up gives wrong limits.

8. Beyond these charts

  • Small, persistent shifts: CUSUM or EWMA charts.
  • Rare events: g chart (items between events) or t chart (time between events).
  • Many short runs or products: Z-MR (standardised) charts.
  • Several streams or heads: separate charts per stream, or a between/within chart.

Stat Studio draws I-MR, Xbar-R, Xbar-S, P, NP, C and U. The charts in section 8 are not in the program.

All sample data in Stat Studio is simulated for training.
