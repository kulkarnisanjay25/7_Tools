How the limits are managed (good practice)
  • Phase 1, baseline: collect 20 to 25 subgroups (or at least 100 readings). Limits are trial limits and update with every point.
  • Review the baseline. Investigate flagged points. Exclude a point from the limits only when you found and removed an assignable cause, never just because it is outside.
  • Lock the limits. They stay fixed while you monitor, so a shift shows up instead of being absorbed.
  • Phase 2, monitoring: every signal means stop, investigate and write a note. Do not adjust the process for points inside the limits.
  • New stage: start one only after a verified, lasting change to the process. Collect a new baseline, then lock again. Old stages keep their own limits.
  • Keep the subgroup size and sampling method the same inside a stage. Control limits are not specification limits.
